Conditions for Democracy

Topic 8: Conditions for Democracy

Sub-Saharan Africa experienced a significant wave of democratisation in the 1990s following the end of the Cold War, with multiparty elections introduced across the continent. Subsequent decades have seen a more mixed picture — some consolidation of democratic norms, but also democratic backsliding, authoritarian resilience, and the persistence of hybrid regimes that combine formal democratic institutions with authoritarian practices. Scholarly debate has moved beyond celebrating or lamenting democracy’s spread to ask more analytically precise questions: under what conditions do democratic elections become meaningful? What sustains or undermines democratic institutions over time? And how should we evaluate the quality of African democracies?

Essay Question

  1. Scholars should not be ‘lamenting the demise of democracy’ nor should they be ‘celebrating its universal triumph’. Do you agree?
