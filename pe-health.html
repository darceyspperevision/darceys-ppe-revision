Health Care

Week 5 • Michaelmas 2026 • Tutor: Dr Richard Povey

Tutorial Essay

✦ Would it be desirable to increase competition between healthcare providers? Should a distinction be made between primary and secondary care? ✦

Reading List

Week 5: Health Care

Efficiency and equity in the provision of healthcare. Uncertainty and information in healthcare and health insurance markets. Alternative healthcare systems and cross-country comparisons. NHS reform: the internal market and after. * = particularly recommended reading in your tutor's Work Scheme.

General Reading

  • *Barr, (2012, 5th ed) Welfare Economics Chapter 10 (on health care).
  • Cutler, D. (2002), “Healthcare and the Public Sector” in The Handbook of Public Economics, Vol. 4, edited by Auerbach, A. and Feldstein, M.
  • Propper, C. (2001), “Expenditure on Healthcare in the UK: A Review of the Issues”, Fiscal Studies, 22(2): 151-83.
  • Stabile and Thomson (2014) “The changing role of government in financing health care: an international perspective” Journal of Economic Literature 52(2): 480-518.
  • Besley, T. and M. Gouveia (1994), “Alternative Systems of Health Care Provision”, Economic Policy, October.

Asymmetric Information

  • *Blomqvist, A. (1991), “The Doctor as Double Agent: Information Asymmetry, Health Insurance and Medical Care”, Journal of Health Economics, 10: 411-32.
  • Weisbrod, B. (1991), “The Health Care Quadrilemma: an Essay on Technological Change, Insurance, Quality of Care and Cost Containment”, Journal of Economic Literature, 29: 523-52.

Choice and Quasi-markets

  • *Martin Gaynor, Carol Propper, and Stephan Seiler (2016) “Free to Choose? Reform, Choice, and Consideration Sets in the English National Health Service” American Economic Review 106(11): 3521-3557.
  • *Propper, S Burgess, D Gossage (2008) “Competition and quality: evidence from the NHS internal market 1991-1996” Economic Journal, 118, pp. 138-170.
  • *Le Grand, J. (2002), “The Labour Government and the National Health Service”, Oxford Review of Economic Policy, 18 (2): 137-53.
  • Charles J. Courtemanche, Ishtiaque Fazlul, James Marton, Benjamin D. Ukert, Aaron Yelowitz, Daniela Zapata (2019) “The Impact of the ACA on Insurance Coverage Disparities After Four Years” NBER Working Paper 26157: https://www.nber.org/papers/w26157

Also flagged in the Work Scheme

  • *Connolly and Munro (flagged in the Work Scheme; no chapters specified).
  • Gaynor, Martin, Rodrigo Moreno-Serra, and Carol Propper. (2013). “Death by Market Power: Reform, Competition, and Patient Outcomes in the National Health Service.” American Economic Journal: Economic Policy, 5(4): 134-66. (Empirical paper not on the departmental reading list.)

Past Exam Questions

  1. The National Schedule of NHS Costs for 2024-25 lists the guide prices for “Complex Hip or Knee Procedures for Non-Trauma Patients” as ranging from £10,478 to £21,441 (depending upon complexity). Using theoretical and empirical arguments, explain the rationale for centrally setting a guide price for such procedures and evaluate the likely consequences of: (a) Funding all procedures from regional NHS budgets (Integrated Care Systems). (b) Requiring primary care providers to directly commission procedures from secondary care providers using individual budgets at fixed prices. (c) Allowing primary care commissioners to renegotiate procedure prices with secondary care providers below the guide price. (2025)
  2. Imagine that a country is considering moving from a retrospective to a prospective payment scheme for doctors working in the public health care system. What would be the main consequences that the government should consider? Based on your discussion, would you recommend this change? (2024)
  3. Explain why adverse selection in health insurance markets can lead to under-insurance of individuals who are less likely to fall ill (low risk individuals). Use a diagram in your explanation. (2023, Part A)
  4. “Health insurance leads to excessive use of medical services!” Discuss this statement and carefully explain what characteristics of the health care system that are important for whether the statement is true. (2022)
  5. What characteristics of health make the organization of health care provision difficult? Use theoretical and empirical arguments to illustrate the difficulties that arise. (2017)
  6. Is marginal utility of income independent of health status? Using a formal model, show how the optimal provision of health insurance depends on your answer. (2016)
  7. What are the advantages and disadvantages of publicly-funded health insurance? (2015)
  8. Should a government be concerned if overall healthcare costs rise? (2014)
  9. Compare the US healthcare system and the NHS with regard to the incentives for over- or underprovision of healthcare that doctors and patients face. (2013)
  10. With reference to either the UK or the US, explain how the health system addresses the adverse selection problem in the provision of health insurance. What are the distributional consequences of this policy response? (2012)
  11. “A policy of social health insurance combined with private provision of healthcare services gives the best of both worlds - equality of access and high quality care.” Explain why you agree or disagree with this statement. (2010)
  12. Does public provision of healthcare improve or worsen the incentives for doctors to act in the interests of patients? (2009)
  13. Would it be desirable to increase competition between health-care providers? Should a distinction be made between primary and secondary care? (2008)
  14. Could the problem caused by asymmetries of information in healthcare be addressed by regulation, rather than by public production and financing? (2007)
  15. What are the theoretical advantages and disadvantages of allowing (i) patients to choose between NHS primary care providers; and (ii) purchasers to choose between NHS secondary care providers? Drawing on these arguments and empirical studies of past internal market reforms, evaluate the principle of choice within the NHS. (2006)
  16. How would you expect the proportion of national income spent on health care to be affected by the organization of a country’s health care system? Is there any evidence to support your conclusions? (2005)
  17. Are Health Maintenance Organisations a satisfactory solution to the problems of market failure in healthcare? (2004)
  18. “[The NHS], which arose historically largely for equity reasons, works because it goes with the grain of efficiency considerations.” (N. Barr, 1998) Discuss. (2003)
  19. Compared with other developed economies, the U.K. spends a relatively low proportion of national income on health. Of this, a high proportion is spent by government. Would a change in either or both of these proportions be an improvement? (2002)
