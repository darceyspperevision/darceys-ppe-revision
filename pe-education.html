Education

Week 7 • Michaelmas 2026 • Tutor: Dr Richard Povey

Tutorial Essay

✦ “The state should fund and organize high school education but not higher education.” Discuss using theoretical and empirical arguments. ✦

Reading List

Week 7: Education

Human capital. Returns to education. Private and social costs and benefits. Policy issues: state versus private financing, equity and family background, school resources. Quasi-markets and educational reform in the UK. Higher education. * = particularly recommended reading in your tutor's Work Scheme.

Core Textbooks

  • *Barr, Chapter 11, 12 (School and tertiary education).
  • *Connolly and Munro, Chapter 20.

General Reading

  • Dutta, J., J. Sefton and M. Weale (1999), “Education and Public Policy”, Fiscal Studies, 20(4) (reprinted in Myles, Miles and Preston).
  • Hanushek, E. (2002), “Publicly provided education” in The Handbook of Public Economics, vol 4, edited by A. Auerbach and M. Feldstein.
  • *Machin, S. and M. Stevens (2004), “The Assessment: Education”, Oxford Review of Economic Policy, Summer. See also articles by Johnson, and Blanden and Gregg.
  • Spence, M. (1973). Job Market Signaling. Quarterly Journal of Economics.
  • Weiss, A. (1995). Human Capital vs. Signalling Explanations of Wages. Journal of Economic Perspectives.

Choice and Quasi-Markets

  • Brighouse, H. (2000), School Choice and Social Justice, OUP, especially chapter 3. The full text of the book is available as an electronic resource on OLIS.
  • Department for Education and Skills (2006), A Short Guide to the Education and Inspections Act.
  • *Gibbons, S., Machin, S. and Silva, O. (2006), “Competition, Choice and Pupil Achievement”, CEE Discussion Paper 56, available at: http://cee.lse.ac.uk/cee%20dps/ceedp56.pdf
  • *Hoxby, C. M. (2000). Does Competition among Public Schools Benefit Students and Taxpayers?. The American Economic Review, 90(5), 1209-1238.

Determinants of Educational Outcomes

  • Chetty, R., Friedman, J. N., Hilger, N., Saez, E., Schanzenbach, D. W., & Yagan, D. (2011). How does your kindergarten classroom affect your earnings? Evidence from Project STAR. The Quarterly Journal of Economics, 126(4), 1593-1660. Non-technical summary: http://obs.rc.fas.harvard.edu/chetty/STAR_Kappan.pdf
  • Angrist, J.D., Lavy, V. (1999). Using Maimonides’ rule to estimate the effect of class size on scholastic achievement. The Quarterly Journal of Economics, Vol. 114, Issue 2, 533-575.
  • See also Hanushek (2002) chapter.

Returns to Education

  • Harmon, C., Oosterbeek, H, Walker, I. (2003), “The Returns to Education: Microeconomics”, Journal of Economic Surveys, 17(2), 115-155.
  • Moretti, E. (2004), “Workers’ Education, Spillovers, and Productivity: Evidence from Plant-Level Production Functions, American Economic Review, 94(3): 656-90.
  • Oreopoulos, P. (2006). Estimating average and local average treatment effects of education when compulsory schooling laws really matter. The American Economic Review, 152-175.

Higher Education

  • *Barr, N. (2004), “Higher Education Funding”, Oxford Review of Economic Policy, 20(2).
  • Haroon Chowdry, Lorraine Dearden, Alissa Goodman and Wenchao (Michelle) Jin, (2012), “The distributional impact of the 2012-13 higher education funding reforms in England”, Fiscal Studies, Vol. 33, No. 2, pp. 211-235.

Also flagged in the Work Scheme

  • *“Funding Higher Education in the UK” (flagged in the Work Scheme; not matched to a specific entry on the departmental list).

Past Exam Questions

  1. How can we identify and measure returns to education? Explain the main difficulties and how we might overcome them. (2024)
  2. The government is considering increasing mandatory education until the age of 21 (which for many pupils would include a 3 year university degree.) Discuss the desirability of this policy and how that might depend on which theory of the drivers of the demand for education we believe to be relevant in this context. Based on empirical evidence which theory is most likely to be true? Discuss. (2023)
  3. Explain the key differences between the human capital theory and the signalling theory of the demand for education. What are the implications of these two theories for the relative size of private vs. social returns to education? (2022, Part A)
  4. The government is considering making the amount of university tuition fees that each student has to pay depend on their own A-level (or high school) performance. How might the choice of education in equilibrium be affected if better performing A-level students pay more than worse performing students? Or alternatively if worse performing A-level students pay more than better performing ones? What happens to the return to education with the two alternatives? (2020)
  5. Consider the example of teenage children of German migrants living in the UK. There is some likelihood that they will return to Germany at some point in the future. How do the private and social costs and benefits of obtaining university education in the UK differ between these teenagers and British natives of the same age? How does the trade-off change if the likelihood of the German teenagers having or wanting to return to Germany increases? (2019)
  6. “Private returns to school education are so high that every person will want to go to school. The state thus does not need to subsidize schools or require attendance.” Discuss. (2017)
  7. Discuss the empirical evidence that limits the applicability of the First Welfare Theorem in the provision of school education. (2015)
  8. Empirical studies estimating Mincerian wage equations (ln wage = α + β*schooling + γ*experience + δ*experience2 + ε) with Ordinary Least Squares have difficulties accounting for the endogeneity of schooling. Describe and evaluate research designs and estimation strategies that could improve over this approach. (2014)
  9. Does competition in the provision of secondary education improve pupils’ outcomes? Use theoretical and empirical arguments. (2013)
  10. “The state should fund and organize high school education but not higher education.” Discuss using theoretical and empirical arguments. (2012)
  11. Is there any convincing evidence to support Charles Tiebout’s claim that people “vote with their feet”? Drawing on this evidence and theoretical critiques, discuss Tiebout’s hypothesis that the provision of local public goods will be efficient. (2011)
  12. To what extent do the private and social returns to higher education differ? What policy conclusions can be drawn from the evidence on returns to education? (2010)
  13. Hoxby (2003) claims that school choice could be “a tide that lifts all boats”. What are the theoretical arguments for policies to increase school choice? Is Hoxby’s claim supported by the evidence? (2010)
  14. What are the implications for education policy of the evidence from many studies, both at aggregate and individual levels, that educational outcomes are not closely related to measures of school resources? (2009)
  15. “It is not surprising that no advanced country has a system of school education which remotely approximates a pure private market.” (Barr 1977). Discuss. (2008)
  16. Assess the effectiveness of the following proposals for improving the educational outcomes of children from poorer households: (i) Increasing the Child Tax Credit; (ii) More funding for schools in poor neighbourhoods; (iii) Increasing the provision of nursery education; (iv) Raising the school-leaving age to eighteen. (2007)
  17. Who should choose which school children attend? (2006)
  18. Does evidence on the determinants of individual educational outcomes provide useful information for policy-makers? (2005)
  19. “Equity is the main justification for government provision of compulsory education. But equity will be the main casualty of UK policies encouraging choice and diversity in schools”. Discuss. (2004)
