Welfare Reform and Inequality

Week 8 • Michaelmas 2026 • Tutor: Dr Richard Povey

Mini-Essays — 750–1000 words for each question

✦ 1. The government is concerned about the rise in part-time work and wants to put incentives into the tax schedule to encourage more full-time work. Its proposal is to give anyone working over 30 hours a week a tax credit of £200 per week, which is withdrawn at the rate of 30% when annual earned income rises above £20,000. (a) How successful is this policy likely to be for different groups in society? (b) Should the amount of tax credit awarded be reassessed each month or each year? ✦
✦ 2. Can a universal basic income avoid the poverty traps often associated with welfare provision, and at the same time alleviate poverty for those in need? ✦

Reading List

Week 8: Welfare Reform and Inequality

* = particularly recommended reading in your tutor's Work Scheme.

General Reading

  • *Barr, N. “Economics of the Welfare State” (5th edition: chs 6, 8, 9) (4th edition: chs 8, 10, 11).
  • Moffitt and Chan (2018) “Welfare reform and the labor market” Annual Review of Economics 10:347-381.
  • Mirrlees, J.A. (1997) “Information and incentives: the economics of carrots and sticks” Economic Journal 107: 1311-1330.
  • Feldstein, M. (2005) “Rethinking social insurance” American Economic Review.
  • Saez, E., Zucman, G. (2016) “Wealth inequality in the United States since 1913: Evidence from capitalized income tax data”, The Quarterly Journal of Economics, Vol. 131, Issue 2.

Policy Reforms

  • Card and Hyslop (2005) “Estimating the effects of a time-limited earnings subsidy for welfare-leavers” Econometrica 73(6): 1723-1770.
  • *Blundell (2016) “Welfare to work: which policies work and why” in: Labour Supply and Taxation, Blundell, Peichl and Zimmerman (eds.), DOI:10.1093/acprof:oso/9780198749806.001.0001
  • *Hoynes and Rothstein (2019) “Universal Basic Income in the US and Advanced Countries” Annual Review of Economics, also NBER Working Paper 25538.
  • Black, Smith, Berger and Noel (2003) “Is the Threat of Reemployment Services More Effective Than the Services Themselves? Evidence from Random Assignment in the UI System” American Economic Review 93(4).
  • Petrongolo, B. (2014) “Tackling long-term unemployed” http://cep.lse.ac.uk/pubs/download/cp416.pdf
  • Verho, J., Hämäläinen, K., Kanninen, O. (2022). Removing welfare traps: Employment responses in the Finnish basic income experiment. American Economic Journal: Economic Policy, 14(1), 501-522.

Past Exam Questions

  1. What problems is Universal Basic Income (UBI) supposed to solve? Would the adoption of UBI be likely to be successful in achieving these goals? (2025)
  2. “The introduction of a universal basic income in the UK would lead to improvements in the labour market and so the government should consider such a reform.” Discuss this statement. In your discussion include a careful explanation of the expected labour supply effects of a universal basic income. (2023)
  3. A government is choosing between adopting either The Earned Income Tax Credit (inspired by the US) or the Working Families Tax Credit (inspired by the UK). (a) What policy would you advice them to choose and why? (Refer to Figure 1 in your answer.) (b) Once they have chosen either the EITC or the WFTC would you recommend that they adopt any other welfare programs as well? Why/why not? [Figure 1: p. 26 of the Readings PDF] (2022)
  4. Should incentives to induce individuals to return to work be means tested? Should the incentives be time limited? (2020)
  5. Are in-work benefits better suited to correct a market failure (such as an inability to exploit returns to experience) or to achieve redistributive goals? How long-lasting would we expect the effects of in-work benefits on labour supply to be?
  6. The government is concerned about the rise in part-time work and wants to put incentives into the tax schedule to encourage more full-time work. Its proposal is to give anyone working over 30 hours a week a tax credit of £200 per week, which is withdrawn at the rate of 30% when annual earned income rises above £15,000. (a) How successful is this policy likely to be for different groups in society? (b) Should the amount of tax credit awarded be reassessed each month or each year?
  7. Can a universal basic income avoid the poverty traps often associated with welfare provision, and at the same time alleviate poverty for those in need?
