Forms of Autocracy I: Civilian Rule — Personal Rule and Party Systems

Topics 2 & 3: Civilian Rule — Personal Rule and Party Systems

Civilian autocracy in sub-Saharan Africa has taken two principal forms: personalised rule, in which power is concentrated in the hands of a single leader who governs through patronage networks and informal institutions rather than formal bureaucratic structures; and dominant-party systems, in which a single party maintains electoral dominance over extended periods. The concept of neo-patrimonialism has been central to scholarly debates, describing a system in which formal state institutions coexist with — and are frequently subordinated to — informal patron–client relationships. A separate but related question concerns how ruling parties sustain electoral dominance through a combination of institutional advantages, resource distribution, and coercion.

Essay Questions

  1. Is the African state best understood as neo-patrimonial?
  2. What explains the long incumbency of many ruling parties in Africa?
