The Politics of Economic Reform

Topic 7: The Politics of Economic Reform

From the early 1980s, the IMF and World Bank made loans to African governments conditional on the adoption of Structural Adjustment Programmes (SAPs) — packages of economic liberalisation measures including currency devaluation, privatisation, trade liberalisation, and cuts to public spending. The outcomes of SAPs in Africa have been deeply contested. Critics argue that the programmes imposed externally-designed reforms without accounting for African political economies, weakened state capacity, and exacerbated poverty and inequality. Defenders contend that poor implementation by African states, rather than the design of the programmes themselves, accounts for their failure to generate sustained growth. This debate raises broader questions about the relationship between politics and economic reform, and about the agency of African states in relation to international financial institutions.

Essay Question

  1. ‘Structural Adjustment Programmes failed in Africa because they were poorly implemented by African states.’ Discuss.
