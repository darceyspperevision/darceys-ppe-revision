Pensions

Week 6 • Michaelmas 2026 • Tutor: Dr Richard Povey

Tutorial Essay

✦ What are the advantages, if any, of pay-as-you-go (PAYG) pensions over fully funded ones? ✦

Reading List

Week 6: Pensions

Consumption smoothing and the life cycle model. Demographics. Overlapping generation model. Private, contributory and tax-based systems. Funded versus unfunded systems. Pension reform. * = particularly recommended reading in your tutor's Work Scheme.

Core Textbooks

  • *Barr, Chapter 7.
  • *Connolly and Munro, Chapter 18.
  • *Hindriks and Myles, 2nd edition, Chapter 23.
  • *Myles, Chapter 14.

General Reading

  • Banks, J. and C. Emmerson (2000), “Public and Private Pension Spending: Principles, Practice and the Need for Reform”, Fiscal Studies, 1-63.
  • *Barr, N. and P. Diamond (2006), “The Economics of Pensions”, Oxford Review of Economic Policy, 22 (1), 15-38.
  • Bozio, A., R. Crawford, and G. Tetlow (2010), “The History of State Pensions in the UK: 1948 to 2010”, IFS Briefing Note BN105, available at http://www.ifs.org.uk/publications/5000
  • Department for Work and Pensions (2006) Security in retirement: towards a new pensions system, available at http://www.dwp.gov.uk/pensionsreform/pdfs/white_paper_summary.pdf
  • Feldstein, M. and J.B. Liebman (2002), “Social Security” in Auerbach, A. and M. Feldstein, (eds), (2002), Handbook of Public Economics, Elsevier.
  • For further analysis of pension reform see the March 2005 issue of Fiscal Studies and the Spring 2006 issue of the Oxford Review of Economic Policy.

Past Exam Questions

  1. What are the benefits of a state-funded pensions system? (2024)
  2. What are the causes of the pensions crisis? Critically evaluate possible solutions. (2023)
  3. What is the golden rule of capital in an economy in steady state? Can a PAYG pension help to achieve it? (2020, Part A)
  4. “Rational individuals will smooth consumption over their lifetime as best suits them. The only role for government in pension provision is to ensure that the earnings and benefits of working-age people are high enough to allow for sufficient saving.” Discuss. (2018)
  5. Explain the difference between fully funded and pay as you go (PAYG) pension systems. If the return on investments exceeds the natural growth rate of the economy, does that mean that fully funded schemes are better than PAYG? What if the rate of return on investments is less than the natural growth rate? (2017)
  6. How should pensions be organised? (2016)
  7. “UK state pensions will be increasingly focussed on redistribution rather than providing a reasonably high replacement rate.” To what extent do you think this is a reasonable policy response to ongoing demographic changes? (2015)
  8. “Since the rich have more savings, capital income should be taxed at least as heavily as labour income.” Do you agree? (2014)
  9. “PAYG public pension systems do serve a useful purpose, which should be salvaged and enhanced by a deeper reform of the Welfare State.” Discuss. (2013)
  10. What are the implications of rising dependency ratios for the (re)design of public policy towards pensions?
  11. How should care of the elderly be funded? Consider the merits of (i) taxes paid upon death, (ii) provision as part of the National Insurance system, and (iii) general taxation. (2010)
  12. What are the advantages, if any, of pay-as-you-go (PAYG) pensions over fully funded ones? (2010)
  13. Discuss the proposition that since a pay-as-you-go pension system does not crowd out private savings, it is to be preferred to a fully-funded system. (2009)
  14. Why not leave pension provision to the market? (2007)
  15. What are the implications of a sustained fall in the real interest rate for both private pension provision and public pension provision? (2006)
  16. Is intergenerational equity the central issue in pension reform? (2005)
  17. “The only sensible response to rising dependency ratios is to encourage increased saving.” Discuss. (2003)
  18. How (if at all) should the government respond to private pension providers replacing defined benefit schemes with defined contribution schemes? (2002)
  19. “The case for fully-funded pension schemes is clear: if the dependency ratio rises, pay-as-you-go schemes require higher contributions to maintain pension provision levels, fully-funded schemes do not.” Discuss. (2000)
