Income Taxation and Labour Supply

Week 3 • Michaelmas 2026 • Tutor: Dr Richard Povey

Tutorial Essay

✦ “Theory suggests that the marginal rate of income tax should be low both at the bottom and at the top of the income distribution, and higher in between. In practice, the marginal rate of the combined tax/benefit system displays the reverse pattern.” Discuss. ✦

Reading List

Week 3: Income Taxation and Labour Supply

Microeconomics of taxation and labour supply; incentive effects of income tax; optimal income tax; empirical evidence on labour supply. * = particularly recommended reading in your tutor's Work Scheme.

Core Textbooks

  • *Atkinson and Stiglitz, Lectures 2 and 13.
  • *Connolly and Munro, Chapters 10, 11 and 13.
  • *Hindriks and Myles, 2nd Edition, Chapter 16.
  • *Myles, Chapter 5.

Overview of the UK tax system

  • Adam, S., J. Browne and C. Heady (2010), “Taxation in the UK”, in the Mirrlees Review available at http://www.ifs.org.uk/mirrleesReview/publications.

Labour Supply

  • Blundell, R. (1992), “Labour Supply and Taxation: a Survey” Fiscal Studies 13 (3), 15-40.
  • Bosworth, B. and G. Burtless (1992), “Effects of Tax Reform on Labor Supply, Investment and Savings”, Journal of Economic Perspectives, 6 (1).
  • Meghir, C. and D. Phillips (2010) “Labour Supply and Taxes” in the Mirrlees Review.

Optimal Income Taxation

  • *Brewer, M., E. Saez, and A. Shephard (2010), “Means Testing and Tax Rates on Earnings”, in the Mirrlees Review. (Your tutor: a recent empirical application of the Mirrlees model to the UK income tax system; the appendix gives a clear technical derivation of the Mirrlees model.)
  • *Heady, C. (1993), “Optimal Taxation as a Guide to Policy: a Survey” Fiscal Studies, 14 (1), 15-41.
  • Mankiw, N. G., M. Weinzierl and D. Yagan (2009), “Optimal Taxation in Theory and Practice”, Journal of Economic Perspectives, 23(4), 147-174.
  • Saez, E. (2001), “Using Elasticities to Derive Optimal Income Tax Rates, Review of Economic Studies, 68(1) 205-229.
  • Diamond, P. and E. Saez, (2011), “The Case for a Progressive Tax: From Basic Research to Policy Recommendations” Journal of Economic Perspectives, 25(4): 165-90.
  • Stern, N. (1987), ‘The Theory of Optimal Commodity and Income Taxation: An Introduction’, in D. Newbery and N. Stern, (eds), The Theory of Taxation for Developing Countries, OUP, 1987.

Past Exam Questions

  1. For the tax year from 6 April 2024 to 5 April 2025, workers in the UK incur a marginal tax rate of 45% on all income over £125,140. Should the marginal tax rate on higher levels of income (e.g. over £1 million) be raised? (2025)
  2. Suppose a technological shift increases productivity differences across individuals in a society. How would this change optimal income tax? (2024, Part A)
  3. Why might a government prefer income taxation to commodity taxation? (2023, Part A)
  4. What factors determine optimal marginal income tax rates? Suppose individuals can move across jurisdictions with different levels of taxes and public good provision. How might the determination of the optimal income tax be affected? (2023)
  5. Suppose there are many groups in society, each with a different degree of lobbying power. The government sets income taxes according to the lobbying power of each group. How would the solution differ from income taxes set by a utilitarian planner? (2022)
  6. What justifies constraining the marginal tax rate to lie between 0 and 1? In what circumstances, and for what incomes, would you set the marginal tax rate close to either extreme? (2020)
  7. The government has decided to simplify the tax system through imposing a single marginal tax rate and providing the same lump sum benefits to all. Explain how to determine the optimal tax rate. (2019)
  8. What is meant by “the social value of government revenue”? What is the “social marginal utility of income”? How should these be related when we have linear income tax and lump sum benefits? (2018, Part A)
  9. (a) Assume that it is never optimal to set a marginal tax rate on income less than zero or greater than one. Explain why, if there is a highest earner, that earner should face a zero marginal tax rate. (b) Discuss the premise of part (a). Could it ever be optimal to set a marginal income tax rate outside the interval [0,1]? (2017)
  10. What possible rationale might there be for having zero marginal tax rates on the very rich and negative marginal tax rates on the very poor? (2016)
  11. What should determine the marginal tax rates on taxable income faced by the rich? Suppose, following a reform to the practices of private bankers, the rich find it more difficult to evade income tax. Should they face higher or lower marginal tax rates? (2015)
  12. What evidence is relevant to determining whether marginal income tax rates are too high or too low? What are the main difficulties in reliably obtaining this evidence? (2014)
  13. How would you evaluate the claim that the rich pay insufficient tax? (2013)
  14. What are the main components of “the theory of optimal income taxation”? What are its main insights? (2012)
  15. What does economic theory teach us about how to determine the optimal marginal tax rates for high income individuals? (2010)
  16. On what information should a desirable income tax system be based? (2009)
  17. Should different groups in an economy face different income tax schedules? If foreign workers are more likely to emigrate if they are taxed heavily, what are the consequences for the optimal income tax schedule that they face? (2008)
  18. Assume that growth on the economy leads to a rise in the modal income. What will happen to the optimal progressivity of the income tax? (2007)
  19. Assess the case for the introduction of a flat income tax in the UK. (2006)
  20. Does the theoretical analysis of optimal income taxation make a convincing case against higher marginal tax rates for higher earners? Is it supported by empirical evidence on labour supply? (2005)
  21. What are the efficiency and distributional gains and losses of raising the marginal tax rate on income between £30,000 and £40,000 per annum? What light does your answer throw on the shape of the optimal income tax schedule? (2004)
  22. Explain how the optimal income tax schedule can be determined. How should tax rates respond to an increase in the inequality of pre-tax incomes? (2003)
  23. “Theory suggests that the marginal rate of income tax should be low both at the bottom and at the top of the income distribution, and higher in between. In practice, the marginal rate of the combined tax/benefit system displays the reverse pattern.” Discuss. (2001)
  24. Outline the considerations in determining the optimal linear income tax. Are there any bounds on how high the optimal rate can rise? (2000)
