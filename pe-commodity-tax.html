Tax Incidence and Commodity Taxation

Week 2 • Michaelmas 2026 • Tutor: Dr Richard Povey

Tutorial Essay

✦ “Commodity taxes should be inversely proportional to demand elasticities.” “Commodity taxes should be higher for goods that are complementary with leisure.” “Commodity taxes should be lower for goods consumed by the poor.” Are these statements true? If so, how can they be reconciled? ✦

Reading List

Week 2: Tax Incidence and Commodity Taxation

Tax incidence; Ramsey taxation; inverse elasticity rule. * = particularly recommended reading in your tutor's Work Scheme.

Past Exam Questions

  1. What are the welfare effects of commodity taxation when leisure can be taxed? When leisure cannot be taxed, what is the rationale for taxing complements of leisure? Can this conflict with equity concerns? (2022, Part A)
  2. Should goods be taxed according to the inverse of the price elasticity of demand?
  3. What assumptions do we need to make to be sure that it is welfare-improving to set all commodity taxes or subsidies to zero? Do these assumptions always hold? (2019, Part A)
  4. In the UK, household heating is taxed at 5%, while most other consumer goods are taxed at 20%. Discuss whether this is a good policy. (2018)
  5. Suppose in country X it is very difficult to tax income (incomes are not observed by the government), whereas in country Y the majority of income is readily taxed at source. Discuss how the optimal commodity tax regimes might differ between the two countries. (2017)
  6. Since the poor have very different consumption patterns than the rich, differential taxes on commodities will necessarily have distributional consequences. Commodity taxes should therefore be designed to have a positive impact on the distribution of welfare. Discuss. (2016)
  7. Is the current UK system of commodity taxation (VAT with exemptions for certain items like food) optimal? Discuss. (2015)
  8. Do you agree that since taxing commodities creates a deadweight loss, commodity taxes should be designed to minimise this loss? (2014)
  9. Should staple food be taxed less heavily than luxuries? If so, why? (2013)
  10. “If there were no other way of transferring resources to the poor, setting low taxes on necessities and high taxes on luxuries might be a good policy. But it is unlikely to be so when other instruments are available.” Explain and discuss. (2012)
  11. What are the main principles of optimal commodity taxation? Are differential commodity taxes useful when income can be freely taxed instead? (2010)
  12. Assume that the government is constrained to leave some goods untaxed. How does this affect the optimal tax treatment of other goods? (2009)
  13. Can outcomes be achieved through commodity taxation that are not possible through other forms of taxation? (2008)
  14. If commodity tax rates are adjusted to take account of environmental damage, should the adjustment be proportional to the damage created? (2007)
  15. How does the design of an optimal commodity tax system depend on the income tax system in use? (2006)
  16. “Commodity taxes should be inversely proportional to demand elasticities.” “Commodity taxes should be higher for goods that are complementary with leisure.” “Commodity taxes should be lower for goods consumed by the poor.” Are these statements true? If so, how can they be reconciled? (2005)
  17. “The Ramsey Rule is redundant once we allow for differences among households.” Do you agree? (2003)
  18. “Labour supply tends to be very inelastic, so the theory of commodity taxation suggests there should be a high rate of tax on wages.” Discuss. (2002)
  19. “The optimal design of commodity taxes depends on the relative weights given to distribution and efficiency. The structure of taxes in the U.K. suggests that more weight is given to the distributional aspects.” Discuss. (2001)
  20. “Commodity taxes in practice tend to be broadly uniform. This suggests that there is scope for improvement.” Do you agree? (2000)
